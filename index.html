§ 1The problem

The sky is a terrible network.

A stratospheric balloon telescope produces multi-gigabyte science images. The data comes down through a NASA TDRS relay satellite on a high-gain link that is narrow and lossy, and the uplink budget goes to commands and telemetry, so the ground can't acknowledge every packet the way TCP needs.

1000 kbps
TDRSS High Gain (Fastest)

The top data rate on the high-gain link.

500 kbps
TDRSS High Gain (Fast)

Half the top rate.

100 kbps
TDRSS High Gain (Slow)

One tenth of the top rate.

Fire and forget.The sender streams every chunk once, blind. It can't wait for acknowledgements that may never come.
Track, don't trust.The ground keeps one bit per chunk and knows exactly what is missing, even after a power cut.
Ask for the gaps.A low-rate side channel requests only the missing chunk ranges. Nothing that arrived is ever sent again.

    § 4The manifest

    One bit per chunk: the ground's to-do list.

    For every file in flight the ground keeps 117.incoming (the bytes, each chunk written at its offset) and 117.parmanifest (the bookkeeping). Click chunks to "receive" them and watch the real manifest bytes change.

    m31_0142.fits.bz2 · 20 chunks

    Click a chunk to mark it received or lost.

    $ xxd 117.parmanifest

    Chunk n lives in bitmap byte n ÷ 8, bit n mod 8, lowest bit first. Each update is written to a temp file and renamed over the old one, so a crash leaves the old or the new manifest, never half of one.

    § 5Files lost whole · new in v0.2

    When an outage swallows a whole file.

    The cleaner repairs files the ground knows about. But if every packet of a file is lost, the ground never learns it existed, while the payload's ledger says it was sent. Reconciliation closes that gap with no acknowledgements from the ground.

    Outage cuts through a file

    The cleaner repairs it.

    At least one packet landed, so the ground has a manifest and knows the file's size. The outage is just one long gap in the bitmap, requested in a single range once the link is back.

    Outage swallows every packet

    Nobody notices. Until now.

    No packet landed, so there's no manifest and nothing for the cleaner to chase. The sender already marked the file as sent and never picks it up again. The same happens to files still queued when the sender restarts.

    1

    The payload downlinks its ledger

    Every 6 h the sender writes files_sent_list_<time>.csv into its first priority folder, so it goes down like any other file: every path, size and queued_at.

    2

    Files land at their payload path

    Each file lands at final_dir + its full path, and only once it is complete. The folder is the ground's record; no extra log.

    3

    The cleaner compares

    Every 6 h it reads the newest snapshot and checks each file: landed with the right size, still in flight, still queued, or queued under 3 h ago.

    4

    Lost files go again

    Anything else was lost whole: it is requested in full, at the back of the queue, at most once per cycle, until it lands.

    Worst case with the defaults, a lost file is requested again about 12 h after it was lost: up to 6 h until the next ledger snapshot, then up to 6 h until the next pass. Shorten both periods to find out sooner.

    § 6Why it converges

    Each pass shrinks the gap by the loss rate.

    missing(k) ≈ N × pk
    5passes to finish
    +25%extra airtime
    155missing after pass 1

    Extra airtime ≈ p ÷ (1 − p): every chunk costs about one send, plus re-sends only for what was lost. The live demo measured +25.4% at 20% loss.

    § 7Built for a flight you can't go and fix

    One binary. Five modes. No restarts.

    Checked twice

    CRC-32 on every packet and every manifest header. A damaged packet is treated exactly like a lost one.

    Power-cut proof

    Chunks are synced to disk before their bit is set, and manifests are replaced atomically. Restart and it resumes mid-file.

    Retune anything, live

    Switch links, change the speed cap or chunk size, pause, or jump the queue over a Unix socket. The TOML config hot-reloads in about a second.

    Science first

    Ordered priority folders decide what goes next, with a separate debug queue so testing never drains live data.

    Multicast-ready

    The receiver joins multicast groups by itself, so switching from a unicast to a relay link needs no ground changes.

    Every file tracked

    A SQLite ledger records every file seen and sent, with CSV snapshots for the ground.
